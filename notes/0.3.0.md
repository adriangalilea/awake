### Changed

- **Right-click and ⌃⌥⌘A now suspend instead of ending.** With claims running, the gesture lets the Mac sleep normally and keeps everyone's intent: your claim, an agent's process watch, a build's timer all wait, and a claim that arrives meanwhile waits too. The same gesture resumes them and starts your claim at the checkmarked duration. Before, "off" ended every claim for good and "on" brought back only yours, so an overnight job lost its keep-awake the moment you toggled, and any agent re-arming behind your back silently undid your "off". Ending claims for good is still there, as the explicit thing it is: `asleep`, or "End all claims" in the menu.
- **The battery floor finishes the job.** Ending claims at the floor only *lets* the Mac sleep; with the lid open, anything else holding an idle assertion (audio on the speakers, a download) kept it awake and it drained from 15% to hibernation at 1%. Now, below the floor, on battery, with no claim of awake's own and the display already dark, awake puts the Mac to sleep itself, and says so.
- **Real notifications** (awake#1). Banners come from a small helper app inside the bundle instead of AppleScript, so they no longer depend on Script Editor's notification permission. macOS asks once, right after install, with a first banner that says what will arrive there.

### Added

- **`awake suspend` / `awake resume`**: the toggle from the shell.
- **The dmg is served from awake.untitled.garden**, and the Homebrew cask points there, so downloads are counted; GitHub keeps a copy of the asset.
